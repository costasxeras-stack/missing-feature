My Garage: say the car once, see what fits everywhere.

In Second Look I logged how Allegro search handles "klocki hamulcowe golf 7": the car in the query barely changed the results, and no card said whether a part fits. This is the feature I would build next. Click through the prototype, then read the spec, the sizing model and the launch plan.

The flow, in five screens

Tap inside the phone, or jump to a screen here. Product names are placeholders and fit labels are illustrative, not checked against a parts catalogue.

Problem

Drivers name their car in the search query, but results do not use it. To be sure a part fits, they must fill in a vehicle filter that the query could have filled, or open each listing. Wrong guesses become returns, and doubt becomes abandoned searches.

Evidence and its limits

  • Observed: for "klocki hamulcowe golf 7", 4 of 60 results on page 1 named the Golf 7, no card showed a fit label, and "golf 9" returned more offers than "golf 7" (Second Look test log, 21 November 2026).
  • Not known: how many searches name a car, how often the vehicle filter is used, and the share of returns for wrong fit. These are [placeholders] in the sizing model.

Users

  • DIY drivers who buy a part a few times a year and know their car model but not the engine code.
  • Small garages buying for many cars, who need several vehicles saved.

Requirements

IDRequirementPriority
G1Detect make, model and generation in the query (numbers, Roman numerals, "mk7", Polish and English) and show it as a removable chipMust
G2Apply the detected vehicle as a filter that ranks fitting parts first, without hiding parts with no fitment dataMust
G3Fit label on every card in car parts categories: fits, fits some engines, does not fit, no fitment dataMust
G4Ask only for the missing detail (usually engine) in a bottom sheet, with "skip"Must
G5Save vehicles to My Garage, signed in or on the device, and pick one in one tapShould
G6Promoted listings follow the same fitment ranking rule as organic onesShould, needs ads team
G7Message when a model is not recognised, with the closest matchShould
G8Fit check on the offer page before "Add to cart"Could

Edge cases

  • Query names two cars ("golf 7 or octavia 3"): show both chips, ask which one.
  • Model names that are also words: detect only in car parts categories.
  • Listing with no fitment data: label "no fitment data", never "does not fit".
  • Seller fitment data conflicts with the catalogue: trust the catalogue, flag to seller.
  • Shopper removes the chip: respect it for the rest of the session.

Non-goals

  • Building a fitment catalogue. This uses the vehicle data the filter already relies on.
  • Changing ad pricing.

Sizing model

Every input is a placeholder to replace with real data. The defaults are round example numbers chosen to show how the model works, not estimates of any company's business.

–extra orders a month from fewer abandoned searches
–fewer wrong-fit returns a month
–monthly value in PLN (margin on extra orders plus return costs avoided)

Launch plan

  1. Offline check (2 weeks). Run the vehicle parser on a sample of [n] car parts queries. Target: [x]% precision on detected vehicles, measured by hand labelling.
  2. A/B test G1 to G4 (4 full weeks). Car parts queries that name a model. Primary metric: search to purchase conversion. Guardrails: wrong-fit returns, revenue per search, promoted listing revenue. Sample size from Test Plan Studio once the baseline is known.
  3. Garage (G5) behind a flag for signed-in users, measured by repeat use of a saved vehicle.
  4. Ads fitment rule (G6) as its own test, agreed with the ads team, with promoted revenue as the main guardrail.

Decision I would ask for

Approve the offline parser check and the A/B test for G1 to G4. Decide on G6 after the first test, with the ads team.
